## Context

Artists exist today only as names: `core/artist_names.py` splits an event's title into names, and `SpotifyArtistLink`, `AppleMusicArtistLink` and `YouTubeArtistLink` are keyed by a normalized name (`core/artist_matching.normalize`). `core/music_ids.py` has a paced MusicBrainz client (1.1 s between requests, retrying 503s, the contact user agent from `core/user_agent.py`), used to read URL relationships. `core/lastfm.py` has a paced Last.fm client with `artist_info(name)`, and `core/artist_evidence.py` the rules `resolve_artists_with_lastfm` uses (a covered state from MusicBrainz's area hierarchy or a Last.fm bio; "shared name" notes in a bio). `core/google_sheets.py` and `sync_venues_from_gsheets` read a sheet tab with a service account on Render and with `gog` locally, keyed by `Venue_ID`.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** records for artists and bands with members; a repeatable import from a sheet or CSV that editors own; MusicBrainz data stored under its licence; Last.fm and Bandcamp used only as their terms allow.

**Non-Goals:** public pages; linking events or the Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube links to records; scheduled runs; reading anything from Bandcamp; storing anything from Last.fm; Discogs or Wikidata lookups beyond storing their IDs.

## Decisions

**1. Two models on one abstract base, and a membership model.** `MusicAct` (abstract) holds the shared fields: `name`, `name_key` (normalized, indexed), `act_id` (the import's `Act_ID`, unique, nullable), `city_town`, `state`, `website`, `bandcamp_url`, `notes`, and the MusicBrainz fields: `musicbrainz_id` (unique, nullable), `musicbrainz_status` (`matched`, `ambiguous`, `not_found`, `editor`), `musicbrainz_checked_at`, `mb_type`, `country`, `area`, `begin_date` and `end_date` (MusicBrainz's partial dates, "1994" or "1994-05", kept as text), `genres` (array), `wikidata_id`, `discogs_url`. `Artist(MusicAct)` and `Band(MusicAct)`. `BandMembership(band, artist, role, begin_year, end_year, source)`, unique on `(band, artist, role)` so one person can hold two roles. Alternative rejected: one `Act` model with a kind field, which the user didn't ask for and which makes "members" a self-relation that can point at a band.

**2. Who owns which field.** Each record has `field_sources`, a map from a field's name to who set it: "import", "musicbrainz" or "editor" (the admin's `save_model` marks the form's `changed_data` as the editor's). The import writes a field of its columns only when it's unset or the import's own; enrichment writes the MusicBrainz fields, and fills `website` and `bandcamp_url` only when they're empty, marking them as MusicBrainz's. (Built as `field_sources` rather than the planned `editor_fields`, which couldn't tell a website MusicBrainz filled from one the import set.) Memberships carry their `source`; the import adds and updates its own but never removes one, and enrichment never removes an import's or an editor's.

**3. The sheet layout.** One header row; columns `Act_ID` (required, e.g. A0001), `Name` (required), `Kind` (`artist` or `band`), `Members` (bands: `Name (role); Name (role)`), `Town`, `State` (two letters), `Website`, `Bandcamp`, `MusicBrainz_ID`, `Notes`. A CSV and a sheet tab are parsed into the same rows (`csv.DictReader`; the sheet's values to dicts by header), so one importer serves both. Members are matched to an artist with the same `name_key`, preferring one already in the band, else created without an `Act_ID`; an artist's own row later takes over that record (when it's the only one with the name and no `Act_ID`), so a member listed before their row isn't duplicated. An editor can merge other duplicates in the admin later. Two records can't share a MusicBrainz ID: when a lookup finds an artist another record already has, the record is marked ambiguous for an editor.

**4. Matching on MusicBrainz.** Search `artist` with the name, type `person` or `group`, and take candidates whose normalized name (or an alias) equals the record's `name_key`. Accept the only one, unless the record has a state and MusicBrainz places the candidate only outside the US, by its area or its begin area: its search is fuzzy, and recording the fixtures (2026-10-29) showed "Horse" returning ten groups, of which the only exact "Horse" is English, which a local Horse not on MusicBrainz would otherwise get. A first version also rejected a candidate in another US state, but the sample run that day rejected the right people: Kim Deal lives in Boston and was born in Dayton, and Mal Blum lives in New York. Acts move, so another state isn't evidence of another act. With several, accept the one whose area is in a covered state (`artist_evidence.covered_places`, following the area's "part of" links as `resolve_artists_with_lastfm` does), else the one whose MusicBrainz ID Last.fm's page gives, unless its bio has a shared-name note. A band's members come from a lookup with `inc=artist-rels+url-rels+genres` ("member of band" relationships, with their `begin`, `end` and instrument attributes as the role); a person's links from `inc=url-rels`. Each record costs one search and one lookup, about 2 s at MusicBrainz's pace: 500 records take about 17 minutes.

**5. Licensing.** MusicBrainz core data is CC0 and is stored (checked 2026-10-29 on musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_Database: artists with their areas and dates, and "relationships & URLs", are core; "tags (including genre associations)" are supplementary). Genres are supplementary data (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0); they're stored, shown only in the admin, and credited "Genres from MusicBrainz (CC BY-NC-SA)". Last.fm answers live only in memory during a run. Bandcamp: only URLs, validated as `https` on `bandcamp.com` or a subdomain, never fetched.

**6. Introductions from MusicBrainz and Wikipedia.** MusicBrainz has no bio: its `disambiguation` is a short note (core data, CC0), kept as `disambiguation`, and its annotation is supplementary free text, often editing notes, which isn't kept. For a bio, Wikipedia: MusicBrainz's own pages show Wikipedia's opening paragraph, found the same way. After a match, when the record has a `wikidata_id`, `core/wikipedia.py` asks Wikidata (`wbgetentities`, `props=sitelinks`, `sitefilter=enwiki`) for the English article's title, then Wikipedia's REST summary (`/api/rest_v1/page/summary/<title>`). It's accepted only when `type` is `standard` and `wikibase_item` equals the record's Wikidata ID, and stored as `wikipedia_extract` (the plain-text `extract`, unmodified, since changing it would make our version a derivative work to license and mark), `wikipedia_url` (`content_urls.desktop.page`), `wikipedia_revision` and `wikipedia_fetched_at`. `thumbnail` and `originalimage` are ignored: an image's licence is its own. Two extra requests per record with a Wikidata ID, paced at one a second like MusicBrainz, with the contact user agent, which Wikimedia's user-agent policy asks for. The admin shows the text with "From the Wikipedia article <link>, under CC BY-SA 4.0 <licence link>", which is the credit Wikipedia's reuse guide asks for; a later public page must show the same. A failed Wikipedia request leaves the record's earlier text and is reported, without failing its MusicBrainz match.

**How it's tested.** Model tests for memberships; importer tests from a CSV string and from mocked sheet values (gog and the service account mocked) for every scenario, including the editor's field and the bad row; enrichment tests with recorded MusicBrainz JSON fixtures in `core/tests/fixtures/musicbrainz/`, recorded Wikidata and Wikipedia answers in `core/tests/fixtures/wikipedia/`, and a mocked Last.fm client, asserting nothing from Last.fm reaches the database and no request goes to bandcamp.com; admin tests for the inline, the hand-set ID and clearing. The suite passes with outbound network blocked.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[Duplicate member artists]** A member named in two bands' rows becomes one artist if the names match, but "Jon Smith" and "Jonathan Smith" become two. → MusicBrainz IDs join them where it knows both; an admin merge action is a follow-up.
- **[A wrong MusicBrainz match]** Small local acts are often missing from MusicBrainz or share names. → Only exact names of the right kind are accepted, and several candidates need evidence; the admin can set or clear the ID.
- **[MusicBrainz is slow or busy]** → The client's pacing and 503 retries; `--limit` for batches; a stopped run keeps what it saved.

## Migration Plan

Migrate. Make the sheet in the layout above, import it with `--dry-run` first, then for real; then run the enrichment with `--limit 20 --dry-run`, check the matches, and run it for the rest. Nothing runs on a schedule yet.

## Later

**Bandcamp's embedded player**, with the public pages. Bandcamp's "Share / Embed" code is an iframe from `bandcamp.com/EmbeddedPlayer/album=<id>/...` (or `track=<id>`). An editor or the act pastes it; the site keeps only the album or track ID, validated as digits, and renders Bandcamp's player itself, so no pasted HTML is stored or shown. Bandcamp serves the player and its music, and no request is made to Bandcamp to find the ID. Nothing in this change depends on it.

## The sample run (2026-10-29)

A sample of 14 Massachusetts acts, imported locally, then `enrich_artists_from_musicbrainz --limit 20 --dry-run` (output in `logs/artists-sample-2026-10-29.log`):
- **Bands:** Dinosaur Jr., Horse Jumper of Love, Krill, Lake Street Dive, Palehound, Pile, Pixies, Speedy Ortiz and The Lemonheads matched the right MusicBrainz groups, with their members. Pile was picked from several groups of that name by its covered begin area, Boston. "Horse" was rightly left ambiguous.
- **Artists:** the first version left Kim Deal, Mal Blum and Sadie Dupuis "not found", rejecting them for living in another state than the sheet said; with the rule above, all three, and J Mascis, match the right people.
- **The import:** a member written "Sadie Dupuis (guitar; vocals)" was split at the semicolon inside the parentheses and dropped silently. Members are now split only outside parentheses, and an entry that can't be read is reported.
- **MusicBrainz was busy** (HTTP 503 after four tries) twice during the runs; each stopped as designed, keeping what it had saved.
- **Introductions** (dry run of 12 records, after task 6): 9 got their English Wikipedia article's opening paragraph, each the right one: Dinosaur Jr., Horse Jumper of Love, Lake Street Dive, Palehound, Pile (`Pile_(band)`, not Wikipedia's "Pile" disambiguation page), Pixies (`Pixies_(band)`), Speedy Ortiz, The Lemonheads and Black Francis. Krill and Ellen Kempner have no Wikidata link on MusicBrainz, so no text, and "Horse" stayed ambiguous. Pile's and Horse Jumper of Love's paragraphs were read by hand: both are the Boston bands.
